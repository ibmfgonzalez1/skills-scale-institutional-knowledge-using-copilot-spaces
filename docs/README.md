# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This documentation describes OctoAcme's end-to-end project management lifecycle: initiate and authorize work, plan scope and delivery, execute and track progress, manage risks and communication, release and deploy safely, and capture learnings through retrospectives and continuous improvement. The approach emphasizes customer value, iterative delivery, clear ownership, measurable outcomes, and psychological safety.

## Project Management Process Summary

OctoAcme begins with **project initiation**, where the team validates the business need, identifies stakeholders and champions, defines success metrics and an initial timeline, captures early risks, and decides whether the work should proceed to planning. During **planning**, the team turns an approved initiative into an actionable backlog by defining acceptance criteria, estimating work, documenting the Definition of Done, identifying dependencies, and agreeing on milestones and a release plan.

During **execution and tracking**, teams use a project board to make work visible across stages such as Backlog, Ready, In Progress, In Review, QA, and Done. Daily standups, weekly delivery syncs, and sprint or milestone demos provide a regular operating rhythm. Project managers coordinate schedules, risks, dependencies, and stakeholder communication; product managers define outcomes and prioritize the backlog; developers build and test the solution; QA validates quality and acceptance criteria; and stakeholders provide input and approvals.

Risk management and communication continue throughout delivery. Teams maintain a risk register, review it regularly, communicate progress and blockers through status updates, and escalate issues from the team to the PM, Product Lead, and sponsor when business impact requires it. Releases require completed acceptance criteria, passing CI and security scans, release notes, smoke tests, and a rollback or mitigation plan. Quality practices include unit, integration, and end-to-end testing, automated linting and security checks, manual QA when needed, staging verification, and post-deployment checks.

After each sprint, release, milestone, or incident, OctoAcme conducts a retrospective. Teams discuss what went well, what could improve, and which two or three action items should be assigned to owners with due dates and success criteria. These actions are tracked in the backlog or issues and reviewed during regular PM syncs, creating a continuous improvement loop that turns delivery experience into measurable process improvements.
